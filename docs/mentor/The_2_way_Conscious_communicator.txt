In his Paramātma-sandarbha, one of the six Ṣaṭ-sandarbhas, Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī systematically presents the nature and qualities of Paramātmā, the Supersoul aspect of the Absolute Truth (Bhagavān), within the framework of Gaudiya Vedānta. The Paramātma-sandarbha specifically focuses on the feature of the Lord who pervades and regulates the material cosmos, distinct from the transcendental, fully personal Bhagavān feature (dealt with more extensively in Bhagavat-sandarbha).
Here are the key qualities of Paramātmā as elucidated by Jīva Gosvāmī:

1. Sākṣī – The Witness
Paramātmā is the constant, impartial witness of all actions performed by the living entities (jīvas). He observes without interfering with the free will of the jīvas, serving as a neutral overseer of their karma.
"The Paramātmā resides in the heart of all beings and witnesses their intentions, thoughts, and actions."

2. Upadrastā and Anumantā – Overseer and Permitter
He is the overseer (upadraṣṭā) and approver (anumantā) of all activities. While the jīva has independence to act, it is Paramātmā who allows or disallows the fruition of actions based on the laws of karma.

3. Antaryāmī – Inner Controller
Paramātmā is present within every living entity and even within every atom (aṇḍa-antara-vṛtti). As Antaryāmī, He controls the functions of the mind, body, and senses, enabling the jīva to function in the material world.

4. Sarvajña – All-Knowing
He possesses infinite knowledge, being omniscient not only of the entire cosmic manifestation but also of the internal realities of every jīva. He knows past, present, and future.

5. Ananta – Unlimited
Paramātmā is unlimited in space, time, and power. He exists simultaneously in every universe and in every atom, yet remains fully present in each place.

6. Iśvara – Supreme Controller
He is the absolute ruler and controller (īśvaraḥ paramaḥ kṛṣṇaḥ) of the material cosmos. All demigods and forces of nature act under His supervision.

7. Kāla-svarūpa – Time Itself
In the context of the material world, Paramātmā also functions as Kāla, or Time, the force that governs creation, maintenance, and destruction.

8. Kṣetrajña – Knower of the Field
According to the Gītā and Paramātma-sandarbha, Paramātmā is the supreme knower of all kṣetras (fields of activity), meaning all bodies and environments. The jīva is the knower of only its own body, while Paramātmā knows all.

9. Bhinnābhinna – Simultaneously One and Different
Jīva Gosvāmī emphasizes the acintya-bhedābheda perspective: Paramātmā is both non-different and different from Bhagavān. He is a manifestation of the same Supreme Person (Śrī Kṛṣṇa), yet functions in a different role, more connected to the material domain.

10. Saguṇa and Nirguṇa
Paramātmā manifests both saguṇa (with qualities) and nirguṇa (beyond material qualities) aspects. Although He pervades the material energy, He is never contaminated by it.

11. Relation to Creation – Creator, Maintainer, Destroyer
Paramātmā directly engages in sṛṣṭi, sthiti, and pralaya — creation, maintenance, and dissolution of the universe — through His three aspects as Brahmā (creator), Viṣṇu (maintainer), and Śiva (destroyer).

12. Aparicchinna – Indivisible and Whole
Though He enters into everything, He remains undivided and whole. This quality is akin to the Gītā’s verse: "He is situated everywhere and yet remains undivided." (BG 13.16)

13. Relationship with the Jīva
Jīva Gosvāmī emphasizes that Paramātmā is distinct from the jīva, yet intimately connected, residing alongside in the heart (dvā suparṇā sayujā sakhāyā – two birds on the same tree). The jīva can attain liberation by recognizing and surrendering to Paramātmā.
